# Hidden-story narrations (written from the lens dossiers; recap-channel voice)

## "The Burglar Who Didn't Want to Hurt Anyone" — Panic Room from Lena's side (~1:45)
He was never supposed to be there with people inside. Lena met Rufus at the card tables, a gambler sunk so deep in
debt that fourteen million dollars sounded like salvation (L150, L167-169). His one condition was simple: not with
people (L138). So when the house turns out to be occupied, he wants out, and only the money keeps him in.

The joke of his life is that he spent ten years building panic rooms to keep people like him out (L242). So his plan
is the gentlest one available: seal the house, wait them out, and keep the stranger with the gun away from the family
(L248-252). When the gas comes, he's the one shouting that they're trying to scare them, not kill them (L291). He's
also the one checking whether anyone actually cut the phone line (L356).

Then the gunman takes over and puts a countdown on Lena's head (L448-462). When the father is dragged in and beaten
in front of the cameras, Lena yells the line nobody else will: his kid is watching (L471). And when the little girl's
blood sugar crashes, he asks one question, hears 'coma, die', and gives her the shot himself (L503-505).

While he does it, we finally hear who he is. He has never done anything like this before (L520). Six years of bad
cards took his house, his car and his wife (L521-523). Woulda been a hell of a dad, he says (L527). He refuses to
kill the witnesses (L576-588), grabs the money and runs (L605-608), and the last thing we hear is police telling him
to put his hands up (L609).

## "The Secret Life of Gunther" — Friends S1-S3 from behind the counter (~1:45)
Everyone in Central Perk sees a guy who pours coffee. They don't know he was once a soap star: Bryce on All My
Children, until they buried him in an avalanche (s02e19), which is why he's the only one who truly understands Joey's
elevator-shaft death. He's also the café's quiet enforcer. One word from Gunther and two thugs give the couch back,
though Chandler thinks Ross played the Gunther card too soon (s02e21).

But Gunther's real story is Rachel. Inside his head it's simple: I love Rachel, I wish she was my wife, and what does
she see in this guy (s03e07)? He knows which of her shoes come in burgundy (s03e08). When Ross asks for a napkin,
Gunther mutters that he already has everything. When Rachel has to be retrained, guess who trains her, and calls her
sweetheart (s03e10). When she quits on him, it's the push she needed to leave waitressing behind.

Then comes the moment the main story never credits him for. Ross runs all over the city trying to stop Rachel from
hearing about the girl from the Xerox place. The person who tells her is Gunther, who innocently asks: was I not
supposed to (s03e16)? A season later, with Ross out of the picture, Gunther finally works up the courage to ask
Rachel to a movie, as her lover, then too out there, and before he can finish, Mark asks her out instead. Gunther's
review of the moment: I dropped a cup (s03e19). He's still bringing her cocoa (s03e21).
